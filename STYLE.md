# Voice & Style Guide

Internal reference. Not published. Used as input for content rewrites.

## Voice

**Smooth, educational, motivational — without being corny.** The reader should feel like they're learning from someone who's been in the trenches and is generous with what they know. High accessibility — never condescending, never gatekeeping.

**Three tests for any sentence:**
1. Would a smart friend over coffee actually say this?
2. Does it teach or motivate without slipping into self-help cheese?
3. Could a small business owner with no tech background follow it?

If any answer is no, rewrite.

## Tone Markers

**Do:**
- Lead with a concrete observation or example, not an abstraction
- Use plain words even for technical concepts
- Acknowledge the reader's situation before prescribing
- Use first-person plural ("we") when speaking as practitioner
- Use second-person ("you") when advising
- Short sentences. Short paragraphs.
- One idea per sentence
- End sections with the takeaway, not a recap

**Don't:**
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Leverage," "synergy," "robust," "seamless," "best-in-class"
- "Game-changer," "revolutionary," "disruptive" (unless literally describing disruption)
- "It's important to note that..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- Empty hype: "AI is changing everything!"
- Long preambles before the actual point
- Bullet-point lists of obvious things just to fill space
- Salesy CTAs ("Don't miss out!", "Act now!")

## Cadence

- Open with a hook — a story, a question, an unexpected fact
- Get to the point within the first 2–3 sentences
- Mix sentence lengths but lean short
- Use white space — let ideas breathe
- Close with something the reader can actually do

## Voice Examples

**Corny (avoid):**
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, businesses must leverage cutting-edge AI solutions to stay ahead of the curve.

**On voice:**
> AI moved from "interesting demo" to "actually useful" sometime last year. If you haven't put it to work in your business yet, you're behind. Here's where to start.

---

**Corny (avoid):**
> Cybersecurity is more important than ever! Don't let hackers ruin your business — protect yourself today!

**On voice:**
> Most small business breaches happen because nobody turned on multi-factor authentication. Five minutes per user. Stops 99% of credential-stuffing attacks. Do this today.

---

**Corny (avoid):**
> ChatGPT is a revolutionary tool that's transforming the way we work and live!

**On voice:**
> ChatGPT is good at three things: drafting, summarizing, and explaining. It's bad at math, dates, and anything where being wrong is expensive. Use it for the first set, double-check it on the second.

## Education Mode

When teaching a concept:
1. **Anchor in something familiar** — analogy or comparison the reader already knows
2. **Show, don't lecture** — example before definition
3. **Name the trade-off** — what does this approach cost? When does it break?
4. **Give a default** — "if you're starting out, do X"

## Motivation Mode

When pushing the reader to take action:
1. **Make the cost of inaction concrete** — what specifically goes wrong if they don't do this?
2. **Lower the activation energy** — "this takes 10 minutes" beats "this is critical"
3. **Validate their starting point** — "if you're like most small businesses..." beats "you should already be doing this"
4. **No moralizing** — describe consequences, don't shame
