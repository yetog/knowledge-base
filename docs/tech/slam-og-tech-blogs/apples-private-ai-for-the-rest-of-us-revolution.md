# Apple's Private AI For The Rest of Us Revolution

**Source:** https://www.wte.net/Blog/June-2024/Apple-s-Private-AI-For-The-Rest-of-Us-Revolution  
**Date:** June 2024  
**Author:** Eric Garrison

---

## Our Take

Apple introduced "Apple Intelligence" at its Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC), positioning AI as a consumer-friendly innovation. The company frames this initiative as a long-term strategic investment, viewing artificial intelligence as fundamental to shaping future products and industry direction.

The approach emphasizes personalization through features like Genmoji, which enables users to craft custom emojis reflecting individual style. Apple distinguishes itself through this user-centric philosophy rather than purely technological advancement.

## Privately Personal

Apple executives Tim Cook and Craig Federighi highlighted privacy as central to their AI development. The strategy relies on on-device processing, keeping most computational tasks local to iPhones, iPads, and Macs rather than routing data to cloud servers.

This architecture reduces data breach risks and maintains user control over personal information. The system learns from user behavior without collecting identifying details — enabling personalization while protecting privacy.

Apple implemented strict data minimization and granular user controls, allowing users to manage app permissions independently. This contrasts with recent industry controversies, such as Adobe's terms-of-service update implying potential use of user-created content for training models.

Siri received enhancements through advanced language models and machine learning, positioning the assistant as more contextually aware. Apple's approach emphasizes accessibility over raw technological capability compared to competitors like OpenAI and Microsoft.

## AI's Killer App?

Apple's inclusivity messaging targets tech enthusiasts, casual users, and business professionals. iOS 18 launches this fall on iPhone Xs and newer, with Apple Intelligence beta available on iPhone 15 Pro models.

The rollout acknowledges device capability variations — newer iPhones showcase full features, while older models like iPhone 12 and 13 access limited AI enhancements. This tiered approach prioritizes inclusive adoption over technological gatekeeping.

Market response proved substantial, with Apple's stock reaching record highs following the announcement. Industry observers from Bloomberg to tech analysts recognized the commercial potential of this accessibility-focused strategy, signaling the beginning of a new competitive phase in consumer technology.
